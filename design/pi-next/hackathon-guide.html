1 Overview

What Pi Next is and the one rule that shapes everything else.

Pi Next is Pi Network's hackathon, launched on 19 November 2026 at the end of the first online Ecosystem Summit. The theme is open: any app that meets real needs, as long as it follows the Mainnet Listing Requirements. Existing apps are allowed; Pi says the console helps developers "bring existing applications to Pi".

Key rule: an entry must be a Mainnet Pi app (or a SoloHost app). The entry form says "Apps on Testnet only aren't shown". The Mainnet listing must be submitted for review. It does not have to be approved before the deadline.

Who can enter

  • Anyone over 18. Pi staff and their families are excluded.
  • Any team size.
  • Up to 3 apps per team.
  • One account per person, or you're disqualified.

Two ways to build

  • Pi app, registered and submitted through the Developer Web Console. This is our route.
  • SoloHost app for Pi Desktop. Not relevant to us.

2 How to register

Each team member does this on their own. The client should be the team lead.

  1. Get a Pi account and the Pi BrowserYou need them on your phone to approve the sign-in.
  2. Open the Developer Web Console on a desktop browserdevelopers.minepi.com/hackathon/register. Signing in from inside the Pi Browser isn't supported yet.
  3. Sign in with PiScan the QR code with your phone camera and approve in the Pi Browser.
  4. Fill in the formPi username (taken from your profile, can't be changed), email (filled in from your Pi profile; updates are sent there), region, "How did you hear about Pi Next?", and tick the box accepting the hackathon rules, Developer Terms and Privacy Policy.
  5. Team lead adds membersOn the app's entry in the Hackathon tab, add each member by Pi username (email optional). Listing someone doesn't register them: they must register first. Only the team lead sees the entry on their dashboard.
  6. Optional: midpoint submissionAn early round for feedback. Details are emailed to people who registered.

Registering doesn't ask for KYC. KYC is needed later, to submit the Mainnet listing.

3 What the submission needs

One team member (the lead) submits in the console before the deadline.

Required content

  • The app: a Mainnet app from "My apps"
  • Demo video link: public (YouTube is fine), 3 minutes or less, showing the final app
  • Team members
  • English name and accurate English description
  • Logo that follows Pi's trademark guidelines
  • Preview images of the interface
  • PiNet integration
  • Privacy policy

What the demo video should cover

  • What the app is
  • A walkthrough of its features and interface
  • Who it's for
  • The problem it solves
  • How Pi is part of the experience

The console won't let you submit until all of these are done

4 Judging and prizes

Six criteria with no published weights, and two kinds of prize.

Judging criteria

  1. Alignment with the Mainnet Listing Guidelines
  2. Pi utility and fit with community and ecosystem needs
  3. Technical completeness and innovation
  4. UI/UX, accessibility and ease of navigation
  5. Long-term utility on the Pi Mainnet blockchain
  6. Uniqueness and distinctive use cases

Community Engagement Awards

150,000 USDT shared by up to 2 teams. Chosen from a mix of the judges' view, real user engagement, retention and community traction. Getting real Pioneers using the app early matters here.

Pi Foundation has also set aside up to 20M Pi to reward Pioneers who use or invite others to new Pi apps.

5 Is Vercel + Railway hosting allowed?

Yes.

Allowed. The rule is "a Mainnet Pi app… submitted via the Developer Web Console". That means the app is registered and submitted there, not hosted there. The console just asks for a public HTTPS frontend URL, and its docs give Vercel as an example.
  • The console's AI coding agent is optional; you can build it yourself with the SDK docs.
  • "My apps" in the web console is a browser view of the same Developer Portal apps (the old portal at pi://develop.pinet.com only works inside the Pi Browser). Existing apps carry over; there's nothing to import.
  • Signing in with the Pi SDK only is fine. Any other login (email, Google…) is not allowed. Our app also makes Pi payments, so it needs an app wallet and server API key.

6 Mainnet listing

You can apply directly. A Testnet app isn't required first.

An app's network is chosen when you create it and can't be changed later: a Testnet ("Staging") app can't be converted. Only Production (Mainnet) apps can be listed. The app goes Draft → Deployed → Under review → Listed.

  1. Create a Production (Mainnet) appIn "My apps", under the client's Pi account (the team lead).
  2. Set the frontend URLhttps://bookkeeping-v1.vercel.app. The domain must not start with "pi".
  3. Verify the domainPut the new key in public/validation-key.txt and redeploy. This also gives the app its PiNet address.
  4. Create the app wallet and server API keySet PI_NETWORK_API_KEY and PI_NETWORK=mainnet on Railway, and VITE_PI_NETWORK=mainnet on Vercel.
  5. Fill in the listing pageSubtitle, description, category, language, 3 screenshots, 1024×1024 logo, privacy policy URL and terms URL.
  6. KYCThe client must at least have applied for Pi KYC in the Pi app before he can submit.
  7. Submit for reviewThat's enough to enter Pi Next. Being approved isn't required.
Review time: Pi publishes none. All it says is "Pi team will review the app", and community reports describe reviews as slow. Aim to submit the listing by mid-December. Entering Pi Next doesn't speed up the review or change the listing. It shows the app under "Pi Next" in the Ecosystem.

Listing rules that apply to us

  • Fully working app with a clean, easy interface
  • Pi sign-in only, and every transaction in Pi (no fiat or other tokens)
  • No sending users to outside websites
  • Collect only the data the app needs

7 PiNet

A public https://<name>NNNNN.pinet.com address that works in any browser, so people without the Pi Browser can see the app.

In the new console the PiNet subdomain is assigned automatically when the domain is verified. It's a console setting, not SDK code. Pi's PiNet requirement is about how the app behaves: an app that forces Pi.authenticate on first visit, or has a landing page that blocks non-Pi browsers, can't use PiNet.

9 Plan: handover and submission together

Do the handover first. Moving the accounts keeps every URL the same, so nothing you submit has to be redone.

Handover: move accounts instead of rebuilding

WhatHowEffect
GitHubRepo Settings → Transfer ownership to the client; he adds you as a collaboratorHistory moves with it; GitHub redirects the old URL
VercelProject Settings → Transfer project to the client's teamSame URL, environment variables and domains
RailwayProject Settings → Transfer to the client's workspaceDatabase and environment variables move with it; same URL
Pi appClient creates the Mainnet app under his own Pi account (KYC)He owns the app and is the hackathon team lead

The client should sign up for each service with his own email and 2FA, and invite you. That way he never has to send you passwords.
